💡 Executive Macro Insights

• Historical Highs & Lows: The wettest all-India year occurred in 1961 (1,717.1 mm), while the most severe nationwide drought was recorded in 1972 (1,146.5 mm) followed closely by 1918, 1965, 1987, and 2002.
• Decadal Oscillations: The mid-20th century (1940s–1960s) represented a wetter multi-decadal regime, whereas the late 1960s and 2000s exhibited elevated drought frequencies.
• Monsoon Reliance: The Southwest Monsoon accounts for over 76.5% of national precipitation, establishing it as the fundamental driver of agrarian productivity and water storage across India.

📖 Methodology, Meteorological Definitions & Data Dictionary

Comprehensive documentation of data sources, calculated metrics, and quality handling rules.

🗂️ Data Sources & Distinctions

Dataset 1 (Historical Observed Time Series):
• File: rainfall in india 1901-2015.csv (4,116 rows × 19 cols)
• Captures 115 continuous years (1901–2015) across 36 IMD Meteorological Subdivisions.
• Represents empirical observed monthly, seasonal, and annual rainfall in millimeters (mm).
• Used for trend analysis, 10-year rolling moving averages, drought frequency, and CV% volatility.


Dataset 2 (District Climatological Normals):
• File: district wise rainfall normal.csv (641 rows × 19 cols)
• Captures standard IMD climatological normal averages across 641 district entries in 35 States/UTs.
• Represents long-term 30/50-year baseline normals for spatial drill-down and intra-state disparity analysis.
• Disambiguation Rule: Compound key STATE_UT_NAME + DISTRICT is used to disambiguate identical district names occurring in multiple states (e.g., Aurangabad in Bihar vs Maharashtra; Bilaspur in HP vs Chatisgarh; Bijapur in Karnataka vs Chatisgarh; Hamirpur in HP vs UP).

📐 Mathematical Formulas & IMD Standards

1. IMD Rainfall Departure (Anomaly %)

Departure (%) = [(Observed Rainfall − Normal Baseline) / Normal Baseline] × 100

IMD Classification:
• Excess: ≥ +20%
• Normal: −19% to +19%
• Deficient (Drought): −20% to −59%
• Scanty (Severe Drought): ≤ −60%


2. Coefficient of Variation (CV %)

CV (%) = (Standard Deviation / Mean) × 100

Measures year-to-year rainfall variability. Higher CV (>30%) indicates erratic climates (e.g., West Rajasthan, Saurashtra).


3. Missing Data Treatment:
• Missing values are preserved as nulls and excluded from aggregations (not treated as 0 to avoid bias).
• 18 missing colonial years in Arunachal Pradesh (1901–1915, 1954–1956) and 5 in Andaman & Nicobar (1909, 1943–1945, 1948) are documented.
